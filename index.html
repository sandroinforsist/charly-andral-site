Cuts through a Sugarcane Plantation

Visual art / Documentary Since 2024 · Work in progress
This body of work explores the sugarcane landscapes of São Paulo as a form of standardized environment — at once aesthetic, economic, and infrastructural. These vast monocultures, shaped by centuries of colonial and industrial logics, produce a paradoxical visual experience: hypnotic, repetitive, almost soothing, yet deeply tied to extraction, control, and large-scale transformation of land. The work seeks to hold this tension, approaching the landscape both as image and as system.

The research unfolds through a combination of fieldwork, photography, drone footage, and satellite image composition, often recombined into speculative visual constructs. Moving between ground-level immersion and distant, cartographic views, the methodology echoes a shift in scale — from embodied perception to systemic abstraction. This research questions how such territories are seen, "artialized", and ultimately naturalized.

andar43: a poetic rainforest atop São Paulo

Experimental space / Immersive installation / Event venue Since 2020 43rd floor, Mirante do Vale, São Paulo
Installed on the 43rd floor of the Mirante do Vale, andar43 transforms a former office into a living, evolving ecosystem. More than 100 native Atlantic Forest species coexist with office furniture salvaged from within the building itself — desks, cabinets, and partitions recovered from vacated units as they are dismantled and converted into housing, then reassembled into a fragile, hybrid architecture.

This pocket jungle unfolds against the mineral vastness of downtown São Paulo, creating a dissonant encounter between urban order and vegetal proliferation. Inspired by Anna Tsing and Donna Haraway, the project explores how life re-emerges within abandoned infrastructures.

Over time, andar43 has hosted exhibitions, performances, children's workshops, gardening sessions, and culinary experiences centered on Brazilian Atlantic Forest ingredients. These activations extend and intensify the installation A selva que nos escapa, turning the space into a platform for collective experimentation.

Before A selva que nos escapa, the space was first inhabited through another poetic intervention, Três paus, três redes, o fogo, selected from a micro-architecture competition titled O monstro que comia sementes, which invited proposals imagining possible transformations of the site.

Ubris, des fêtes divines et dérisoires

Site-specific performative parties 2015–2017 Abidjan, Côte d'Ivoire
Six performative rave parties, each exploring a dimension of hybris — ambition, transgression, the desire to exceed human limits. Narratives, performances, and scenographies are anchored in specific sites across Abidjan, creating frictions between theme and context. Each event is a temporary suspension of order, an invitation for participants to inhabit and exhaust their own excess.

The parties — Nocturne au musée, Cyber-pygmées, Divine Vidange, Incitatus, and others — weave together music, costumes, spatial interventions, and collective rituals. They unfold in venues chosen for their symbolic resonance: a museum after dark, an industrial warehouse, an abandoned villa. Each site becomes a stage for excess and catharsis, where the boundaries between performer and audience dissolve.

Ubris sits at the crossroads of relational art, performance, and nightlife culture. It questions the rave as a form of contemporary ritual — an arena where bodies, sounds, and spaces collide to produce something both divine and absurd.

Deriva "nas entranhas do Mirante"

A series of 4 short documentary films São Paulo, 2020
A drift through the corridors of Mirante do Vale, a downtown São Paulo building that stood as Brazil's tallest for over fifty years. Sensitive exchanges with occupants, workers, investors, architects, and residents reveal a structure suspended between ruin and renewal.

Inspired by participatory inventory methods, the narratives are co-constructed with long-time users, whose voices shape the building's living archive.

Juntos no efêmero

Proposal of memory plaques Inventário da Memória Paulistana São Paulo, 2020
The "Memory Plaques" competition was launched in 2019 by São Paulo's Department of Historical Heritage as part of the Inventário Memória Paulistana, an initiative aimed at identifying and safeguarding places significant to the city's collective memory and to the diverse social groups that shape it. Participants were invited to propose short entries (verbetes) — concise narratives to be inscribed on plaques installed in public space, each capturing a fragment of urban memory.

My contribution focused on sites that fall outside of official or monumental history, engaging instead with more fragile, everyday, or overlooked narratives. The texts deliberately avoided academic language, seeking to convey the singular tone and lived atmosphere of each place. Titled Juntos no efêmero (Together in the Ephemeral), the project proposes a situated, subjective approach to memory, one that resists fixation and embraces the transient nature of urban experience.

Three out of six proposals were approved by the jury of the competition.

Charly Andral Osbourne

Visual artist and spatial researcher based in São Paulo, Brazil. Working across digital collage, installation, performance, and documentary, his practice explores the tensions between ecological systems, urban infrastructure, and collective memory.

His work often operates at the intersection of landscape and politics — examining how territories are shaped, perceived, and naturalized through extraction, monoculture, and colonial logics. Central to his approach is a commitment to site-specific engagement, participatory processes, and the activation of overlooked spaces.

Since 2020, he has been developing andar43, an experimental space on the 43rd floor of the Mirante do Vale building in downtown São Paulo — a poetic rainforest where native Atlantic Forest species coexist with salvaged office furniture.
